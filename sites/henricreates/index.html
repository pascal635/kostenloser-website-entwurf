THE PROBLEM

Why most SaaS product videos
do nothing.

It is almost never the animation. It is what happened before the animation.

It explains features, not the problem

Most videos list what the software can do. The viewer is asking what it solves for them. A video that opens with features loses in the first five seconds.

It arrives too late

The industry standard is three to six weeks, and often five to seven from script approval. When your launch date is fixed, the video is ready exactly when nobody needs it anymore.

It looks like everyone else's

Some vendors reassemble prefab graphics. Their videos are all cut from the same cloth. What you get is not a product video, it is a template with your logo on it.

PROCESS

How a project runs.
Four steps, four days on average.

You approve the storyboard before anything gets animated. Expensive changes happen while they are still cheap.

1 Day 0

Call and brief

We spend 30 minutes on your product, your audience and your date. After that you get a fixed price and a fixed delivery date, not a range.

From you: access to the product or a demo, plus your logo and fonts.

2 Day 1

Script and storyboard

I write the script and lay out the film scene by scene. You read it and approve it. This is where things get rebuilt, not later.

From you: one approval, or your changes in one go.

3 Day 2 to 3

Animation

The animation gets made. You get progress updates without having to ask for them.

From you: nothing. I will keep you posted.

4 Day 4

Sound and handover

Sound design, polish, export in every format you need. You get the finished videos and the project files.

From you: your final notes. After that it is yours.

Four days is the average with a clear brief and same day feedback. For larger projects with multiple language versions we agree a separate date up front.

“You work alone. What if you get sick?”

Fair question if a project runs three months. At four days there is no window for anything to go quiet in. You get a fixed delivery date and my phone number before we start. If I cannot hold a date, you hear it before the date passes, not after.

2

“Fast means sloppy.”

Four days is not less work. It is fewer meetings. The animation takes as long as it takes. What other people add on top is approval chains, review rounds and waiting time between departments. None of that exists here.

3

“A studio has more capacity.”

More people means longer here. At a studio your feedback goes through a project manager, an art director and then to the animator. Three handovers, each one costs days. That is also why I take only two projects at a time: so yours is not sitting in a queue.
